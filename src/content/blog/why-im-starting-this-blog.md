For more than ten years, I've been a quiet consumer of the Microsoft business applications community. When I hit a wall in Dynamics, the answer usually came from someone else's blog post, a forum thread, a user group session, or a conference talk. Someone had been there first, and they took the time to write it down.

I never wrote anything down myself. That changed at Community Summit North America this year.

## The moment it clicked

I presented several sessions at Summit this year, on topics ranging from moving D365 F&O source control to Git to using AI coding tools for X++. Somewhere in that week it clicked: if something I write helps even one person get past a hurdle, or see a problem from a new angle, it's worth doing.

Plenty of people in this community have done exactly that for me. It's time I returned the favor, and joined the conversation instead of just following it.

## What you'll find here

- **Real scenarios first.** Every guide starts with the problem I was actually trying to solve, so you can judge whether it fits yours before you read the how-to.
- **D365 F&O depth.** Development, architecture, integrations, performance, and the ALM work that keeps teams moving.
- **AI in practice.** AI coding tools for X++, AI-assisted configuration, and agents: what works today, what doesn't yet, and where humans still need to stay in the loop.
- **Where business applications are heading.** Headless architectures and the broader Microsoft stack, including Power Platform and Azure.

## What you won't find

- **Client names or confidential details.** Scenarios are generalized; the lessons are what matter.
- **Sales pitches.** This is a personal blog, and the opinions are my own.

## What's next

First up, I'm turning my Summit sessions into written guides, starting with migrating D365 F&O from TFVC to Git, including the lessons we learned the hard way.

If there's a topic you'd like me to dig into, or you see something differently, I'd like to hear it. Come say hi on [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/jamesptorres/).

Thanks for reading,\
James
